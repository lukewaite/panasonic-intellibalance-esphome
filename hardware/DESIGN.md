# Inline interface board — design (rev A)

An ESP32-C6 board that sits in the 4-wire link between the ERV and the FV-SW20VEC1 wall control.
It powers up in **hardware passthrough** (a relay connects the original wires straight through)
and lets firmware switch into **intercept** mode, where the ESP drives each line itself.

Status: **built and installed** (board 1, since 2026-10-29): both lines decode cleanly and
intercept controls the ERV. The 110 × 56 mm 2-layer PCB (notched corners, sized for a Hammond 1591C
ABS box) is generated by `kicad/gen/gen_sch.py` and `gen_pcb.py` and autorouted with Freerouting;
ERC and DRC clean; JLCPCB files in `kicad/fab/`. Component values were calculated from the
2025-12-14 capture. The bench checks and the analog capture of the installed board are still to do
([STATUS.md](STATUS.md) "Not done yet").

## Requirements

1. **Fail-safe.** No power, crashed firmware, or ESP in reset → the link behaves exactly as if the
   board weren't there.
2. **Read both lines in either mode.** Passthrough plus logging is useful on day one: it replaces
   Saleae sessions for working out what the registers mean.
3. **Drive both lines in intercept mode** at levels the far end accepts.
4. **Doesn't draw from the ERV's 5 V.** The board mounts beside the ERV and runs from its own
   USB-C. The bus 5 V only passes through to the wall control.
5. **Assembled by JLCPCB** (Economic PCBA, top-side SMD only). Every SMD part is a JLCPCB *basic*
   part except the ESP module and the USB-C receptacle. The few through-hole parts (relay,
   terminals, slide switch, probe header) are hand-soldered by the owner.

## Installation context

- The ERV is a **metal enclosure**. The thermostat wire leaves through its side. The board mounts
  **on top of** the enclosure, between the ERV's terminal block and the wall-control run.
- The Thread border router is in the same room, on the far side of the ERV. From on top of the
  enclosure the board has line of sight to it, so the module's **built-in PCB antenna** is enough.
  The antenna end of the board should overhang or sit at the edge, not lie flat on the metal lid
  (use standoffs), and point away from the enclosure's bulk.

## Block diagram

```
  TO ERV (J1)                                                   TO WALL CONTROL (J2)
  5V  ─────────────────────── bus 5V, passes straight through ───────────────── 5V
  0V  ───────────────────────────── GND (board ground) ──────────────────────── 0V

                         K1 pole A
  RXD ──────────────── COM      NC ───────────────────────┬──────────────────── RXD
  (unit input)                  NO ─── Q2 drain (1k↑5V)   │              (panel output)
                                              ▲           └─► tap ─► U2A ─► ESP RX1
                                         ESP TX1                (commands)

                         K1 pole B
  TXD ─────────┬────── NC       COM ──────────────────────────────────────── TXD
  (unit output)│                NO ─── Q3 drain (2.2k↑5V)               (panel input)
               │                              ▲
               └─► tap ─► U2B ─► ESP RX0  ESP TX0
                       (state)

  K1 coil ◄─ Q1 ◄─ ESP GPIO (100k pulldown) ── SW1 "FORCE PASSTHROUGH"
```

The taps sit on the **sending** end of each line (the wall control's RXD output and the ERV's TXD
output), so they read live traffic in both relay positions. The relay's moving contact (COM)
always faces the **receiving** end, so in intercept that end hears only the ESP.

## Stages

### Relay — K1

- **Omron/Aratas G6S-2 DC5** (DPDT, 5 V coil, 28 mA, THT, DigiKey Z2615-ND), hand-soldered.
  The PCB uses the G6S-2 footprint.
- Resting (NC) = passthrough. Passthrough needs nothing about the link to be understood, because it
  is a direct metal contact.
- Driver: **Q1 AO3400A** N-MOSFET, **D1 1N4148W** flyback, **100 kΩ** gate pull-down, **100 Ω**
  gate series. Q1 is driven from a GPIO with **no internal pull-up at reset** (see the ESP pin
  table). GPIO18–23 have a ~45 kΩ pull-up during reset, which against 100 kΩ would put ~2.3 V on
  the gate and energise the relay whenever EN is low.
- **SW1** (SPDT slide switch, THT) in series with the coil: physical override to passthrough.
  **D1 sits directly across the coil terminals**, with SW1 outside the coil–D1 loop, so opening
  SW1 under load freewheels through D1 instead of arcing the switch.
- **LED2** + 1 kΩ across the coil: "INTERCEPT" indicator.
- Firmware rule: switch only when **both** lines are in their idle gap (RXD ≈ 51 ms, TXD ≈ 81 ms
  per 200 ms cycle). The two sides drift against each other with a beat period of ~400 s, so a
  shared gap of ≥10 ms exists for only ~56 % of phases and can be absent for ~3 minutes at a time.
  Switching is rare (entering or leaving intercept), so firmware waits for a shared gap, with a
  timeout after which it switches anyway and accepts one damaged frame per line (BCC fails, so the
  receiver should discard it; unproven). Two SPDT relays on separate GPIOs would remove this
  constraint; not worth it for rev A.
- **Hung firmware:** a hang with the relay energised would leave both receivers idle. Rev A relies
  on the ESP-IDF task watchdog (ESPHome's loop watchdog resets the chip, and reset drops the
  relay). A hardware keep-alive (relay held only while firmware toggles a pin through a charge
  pump) is a rev B option.

### Receive taps — U2 (LM393DR2G, dual comparator)

Runs from board 5 V. Its open-collector outputs pull up to 3.3 V, which takes care of the level
shift. LM393 inputs tolerate voltage independent of V<sub>CC</sub>, so a live bus with the board
unpowered back-feeds nothing.

Per channel:

| Part | RXD tap (U2A) | TXD tap (U2B) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input divider | 100 k / 100 k | 100 k / 100 k | ÷2 → max 2.5 V at the pin, within the LM393 common-mode range at 5 V. 200 kΩ load on the line (≤25 µA). |
| Input filter | 100 pF to GND | 100 pF to GND | τ ≈ 5 µs, against spikes; small next to the link's own ~80 µs slew. |
| Reference divider (3.3 V) | 22 k / 12 k | 51 k / 10 k | Sets the threshold at mid-swing. |
| Hysteresis R<sub>f</sub> (out → V+) | 220 k | 220 k | |
| Output pull-up to 3.3 V | 10 k | 10 k | |

The line goes to **V−** and the reference to **V+**, so the comparator also inverts: an idle line
(0 V) gives a high output, which is a normal idle-high UART to the ESP.

The ÷2 divider is on both channels, even though TXD normally peaks at 2.3 V. In intercept mode the
ERV's TXD sees only this tap. If the 2.3 V turns out to be a loaded level, the open-circuit level
could be 5 V.

Calculated switching points, referred to the line:

| | line swing | rising threshold | falling threshold | centre | mid-swing target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RXD | 0–4.7 V | 2.47 V | 2.25 V | 2.36 V | 2.35 V |
| TXD | 0–2.3 V | 1.28 V | 1.04 V | 1.16 V | 1.15 V |

All values are JLCPCB basic parts (18 k and 270 k, used in an earlier draft, are extended).

The decoder showed the link decodes cleanly over a wide timing window, so these don't need
trimming. If a measured level turns out different, change the reference resistors.

### Drivers — Q2, Q3 (AO3400A, open-drain inverter with pull-up to 5 V)

Same MOSFET as the relay driver, so no extra part type at JLCPCB.

- ESP TX → 100 Ω → gate, 100 kΩ gate pull-down, from GPIOs with no pull-up at reset (same reason
  as Q1). Drain pulled up to 5 V; drain goes to the relay's NO contact.
- Inverting: ESP UART idle (high) → MOSFET on → line 0 V, which is the link's idle. Start bit (low)
  → MOSFET off → pull-up drives the line high. Firmware uses a plain, non-inverted UART.
- The pull-up is also the source resistance, standing in for the original series resistor.
  With ~1.5 nF of cable (say 30 m of thermostat wire), τ is 1.5 µs with 1 kΩ and 3.3 µs with
  2.2 kΩ, both negligible against a 208 µs bit.
- Idle current: 5 mA (1 kΩ) and 2.3 mA (2.2 kΩ) whenever the UART idles high, in either relay
  position. Harmless (≈35 mW total), and it keeps the firmware simple.
- ESP in reset or unpowered: gate pulled low, drain high, but the relay is off so the drain sees
  only the open NO contact.

Output networks (footprints chosen so values can be changed after the bench test):

| | Pull-up to 5 V | Shunt R to GND | Default fit | Expected level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 → ERV RXD | R<sub>u1</sub> **1 k** | R<sub>p1</sub> DNP | 1 k / open | ≈ 4.7 V into the ERV input, matching the wall control |
| Q3 → panel TXD | R<sub>u2</sub> **2.2 k** | R<sub>p2</sub> DNP | 2.2 k / open | to tune at bring-up to ≈ 2.3 V at J2 |

With a shunt fitted, the low level is still 0 V (MOSFET on), and the high level is the divider.

TXD is the unknown. The wall control has a transistor stage near CN2 (Q3, with R21 2.0 k,
R22 510 Ω, R30 2.2 k, R31 3.3 k around it), which may be the TXD input stage, so the 2.3 V may be
the ERV's 5 V divided against it. No traces are visible in the photo, so this is a guess. Even
5 V through 2.2 kΩ is at most ~2.3 mA into the panel, very unlikely to harm it, but **bring-up step 4
(measure before intercept) is mandatory**. At bring-up,
measure the level at J2 TXD with the wall control connected and pick R<sub>u2</sub>/R<sub>p2</sub> to land
near 2.3 V.

### Dummy loads (DNP by default)

- **R<sub>dl1</sub>**: panel RXD → GND. In intercept, the wall control's RXD output drives only the
  200 k tap instead of the ERV's input.
- **R<sub>dl2</sub>**: ERV TXD → GND. Same for the ERV's TXD output.

Fit these only if one end behaves differently in intercept (e.g. shows a fault). Either end may be
sensing the other's load.

### Protection

- The divider and pull-up/series resistors already limit fault current into the active parts.
- No dedicated ESD diodes on the bus lines in rev A (dropped to avoid an extended part type, and
  because in passthrough their leakage would hang on the original link). The wall control already
  has protection on its side; the board is a few cm of wire from the ERV.

### Power

- **On-board USB-C receptacle** (TYPE-C-31-M-12, 16-pin), power + USB data. CC1 and CC2 each get
  a **5.1 kΩ** pull-down so a USB-C charger supplies 5 V. D+/D− go straight to the ESP's native
  USB pins (GPIO12/13) for flashing and logs.
- VBUS = board 5 V. USB-C shield to GND.
- **AMS1117-3.3** regulator to 3.3 V. Its datasheet specifies a ≥22 µF tantalum output capacitor
  for stability; use **22 µF + 10 µF ceramic** at the output (ceramic-only usually works, but isn't
  guaranteed — check at bring-up), 10 µF at the input, and **10 µF + 100 nF right at the module's
  3V3 pin**. Headroom: USB minimum 4.75 V − ~1.1 V dropout = 3.65 V, enough at the C6's peaks
  (354 mA WiFi TX, 305 mA 802.15.4 at 20 dBm).
- Board-side 5 V load besides the regulator: relay ~28 mA + LED ~3 mA + LM393 ~1 mA + drivers
  ≤7 mA ≈ 40 mA.
- **Bus 5 V is not connected to board 5 V.** It runs J1 → J2 as a wide trace.
- **JP1** (solder jumper, open): bus 5 V → board 5 V through **D6 SS14**, for a future USB-less
  install. Leave open while the unit's 5 V budget is unknown. Never close it with USB connected:
  there is no diode in the VBUS path, so the bus would back-feed the USB port. Via JP1 the 3.3 V
  rail is marginal (5 − 0.45 − 1.1 ≈ 3.45 V at peak).
- **LED1** + 1 kΩ on 5 V: power indicator.
- Bus 0V **is** tied to board GND (the taps need a common reference). Typical USB chargers are
  isolated, so this doesn't create a loop.

### ESP — U1 (ESP32-C6-WROOM-1-N8)

- **ESP32-C6-WROOM-1-N8**, PCB antenna, 8 MB flash. JLCPCB stock checked 2026-10-10:
  **C5366877**, US$4.22, 2,592 in stock. 8 MB leaves room for esphome-matter with OTA.
- Layout: module top 0.25 mm from the top board edge, right edge 1.5 mm from the right board edge
  (Espressif allows ≤2 mm on the feed side). Copper keep-out on all layers over the antenna region
  extending **15 mm** to its left (x ≥ 50.5 mm, y < 7 mm). No tracks under the module body (pour
  and vias only), 4 vias in the EPAD grid, solid GND pad connections, and a via fence along the
  keep-out's lower edge. Relay, screw terminals and USB-C are on the far side of the board.
- Mounting: **≥15–20 mm standoffs** above the enclosure's sheet metal (closer detunes the PCB
  antenna), antenna end toward the border router. Plastic enclosure, if any.
- Fallback if reception turns out poor: ESP32-C6-MINI-1U (u.FL, external antenna), JLCPCB
  C20627095, 4 MB flash. Different footprint, so this would be a respin.
- The C6's 802.15.4 radio runs Thread via ESPHome's `openthread` component (needs a border
  router), and DavidvtWout/esphome-matter lists the C6 as tested. A Thread device that stays powered
  is a full Thread device, so it also strengthens the mesh.
- Support: EN pin 10 kΩ pull-up + 1 µF to GND (Espressif's reset RC). No BOOT/RESET buttons
  fitted: the native USB-Serial-JTAG can reset into download mode. Leave unfitted pads for both
  buttons and a test pad on GPIO9 (boot strap) in case the first flash needs them.
- Use both hardware UARTs, 4800 8E1, **no inversion in firmware**. Pin choice avoids the strapping
  pins (GPIO4, 5, 8, 9, 15), the USB pins (GPIO12, 13) and the ROM boot-log UART (GPIO16/17).
  **Every pin that drives a MOSFET gate has no internal pull at reset** (GPIO2, 3, 10, 11 are
  input-only at reset; GPIO18–23 have a weak pull-up during reset and must not drive a gate).
  The WROOM-1 footprint's pins 1–14 are its left column, facing the comparators, so most signals
  use that side:

| Signal | GPIO | Module pin | Reset state | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UART1 RX | 1 | 9 (left) | — (input) | commands from wall control (panel RXD tap) |
| UART1 TX | 10 | 11 (left) | no pull | commands to ERV (Q2) |
| UART0 RX | 0 | 8 (left) | — (input) | state from ERV (unit TXD tap) |
| UART0 TX | 3 | 26 (right) | no pull | state to wall control (Q3) |
| Relay | 11 | 12 (left) | no pull | high = intercept |

Logs go over USB-Serial-JTAG (`logger: hardware_uart: USB_SERIAL_JTAG`), so both UARTs are free.
Firmware must set `esp32: flash_size: 8MB`; ESPHome otherwise assumes a 4 MB partition table.

### Connectors and test points

- **J1 "ERV"** and **J2 "WALL CONTROL"**: fixed 4-position 5.08 mm screw terminals (THT,
  hand-soldered). Pins 1–4 carry bus 5V, GND, RXD (commands) and TXD (state), but are printed
  `5V 0V TXD RXD`: the ERV's and wall control's own terminal labels, which are the reverse of this
  repo's line names (CLAUDE.md "Wiring"), so the board wires label-to-label. J3's pRX/eRX/eTX/pTX
  keep the repo's names: pRX/eRX are on the pin printed `TXD`. Boards from the 2026-10-10 order are
  printed `5V 0V RXD TXD` and are wired crossed.
- **J3**: 1×8 2.54 mm header (THT): GND, 3.3 V, the four bus nets (panel RXD, ERV RXD, ERV TXD,
  panel TXD) and the two driver drains (Q2, Q3), so the intercept outputs can be checked with the
  relay still in passthrough. For clipping on the Saleae.
- 4× M3 mounting holes for standoffs screwed through the floor of a **Hammond 1591C** (e.g. 1591CSBK). The board is centred in the box and its corner notches clear the lid-screw bosses. Use ~12 mm standoffs, drill wire and USB-C holes in the long wall next to the terminals, and keep the box off bare metal so the antenna sits ≥15 mm above it. Silkscreen labels each terminal pin, each J3 pin
  (GND 3V3 pRX eRX eTX pTX dRX dTX), the SW1 positions (AUTO / PASS — verify with a meter that the
  AUTO side connects pins 1–2), JP1 ("NOT WITH USB") and the EN/BOOT pads.

## Bring-up

Order of operations, each step safe to stop at. [STATUS.md](STATUS.md) has the hands-on checklist.

1. **Solder and meter-check, unpowered.** J1↔J2 continuity through the relay's NC contacts, no
   shorts, and which SW1 side is AUTO.
2. **Install unpowered, capture.** No USB, no firmware: the relay rests in passthrough. The system
   must work exactly as before, which proves the NC path and that the taps don't load the link.
   Capture pRX/eRX/eTX/pTX on J3 with the Saleae. eTX's level, at the ERV end on a clean ground,
   settles the 2.3 V question and the R<sub>u2</sub>/R<sub>p2</sub> choice; if it is well above
   2.3 V, revisit the TXD tap reference before powering.
3. **Bench, no ERV.** Power from USB. Check 5 V/3.3 V, flash over USB, relay clicks on GPIO11,
   SW1 overrides it, LED2 follows. **Hold EN low (reset) and confirm the relay stays off** and both
   driver drains sit high. Scope the 3.3 V rail during Thread transmit for LDO stability. Feed each
   tap from a bench supply: comparator flips at the tabled thresholds.
4. **Passive firmware on the ERV.** Both UARTs should decode with 100 % BCC/parity, like
   `tools/decode.py` does. Publish every register change to Home Assistant. **This is the
   register-mapping tool:** use the wall control normally, read the history. Optionally unplug the
   wall control briefly and capture ERV TXD open-circuit.
5. **Intercept, idle only** (the firmware's "Intercept test" button): the ESP sends the wall
   control's idle frames for 3 cycles. Neither end should notice. If one shows a fault, fit the
   dummy loads.
6. **Intercept, inject**: the Power control sends the wall control's turn-on snapshot for
   3 cycles, then idle, as the wall control does.

## Firmware

`firmware/ervlink.yaml`. The relay stays off (passthrough) and both UARTs only listen, except for
short intercepts: the ESP waits for a gap in both lines' traffic, switches the relay in, sends the
wall control's frames itself for 5 cycles (3 with the command, then 2 idle, as the wall control
does), and switches back out in the next gap. Nothing is forwarded; during the ~1.4 s the wall
control hears nothing and simply shows the ERV's state once the relay is out.

# CLAUDE.md

## What this repo is

Reverse-engineering of the low-voltage serial link between a **Panasonic Intelli-Balance 200
ERV** and its **FV-SW20VEC1 wall control**, with the goal of building an ESPHome-based inline
interface (ratgdo-style) for Home Assistant.

It holds logic-analyzer captures, a decoder, and the rev A inline interface board (ordered
2026-10-10, see `hardware/STATUS.md`). Rev A is installed inline on the ERV and running
ESPHome firmware (`firmware/ervlink.yaml`) over Thread since 2026-10-29. It decodes both lines and,
since 2026-10-30, can intercept and turn the ERV on from Home Assistant.

> **Hardware arriving / bring-up questions → read `hardware/STATUS.md` first.** It records what was
> ordered and the step-by-step arrival checklist.

### Running the decoder

```bash
./tools/decode.py --edges captures/2025-12-14/derived --start 6.8            # instant
./tools/decode.py --edges captures/2025-12-14/derived --start 6.8 --changes  # register deltas only
./tools/decode.py captures/2025-12-14/analog.csv --save-edges captures/2025-12-14/derived
```

The last form re-extracts from analog (~15 s for 26.9M samples) and needs no tuning: thresholds
are **measured per channel from the capture itself** (0.5 % / 99.5 % rails, hysteresis at ±10 % of
swing around mid-point), and columns are matched by Saleae channel name. It reports the detected
rails and the measured bit time, then per-line byte/parity/stop/BCC/frame counts.

**Anything below 100 % on all of those means the parameters or the capture are wrong, not that the
bus is noisy.** The tool says so explicitly when the decode isn't clean.

Margins are wide, so don't fiddle: the decode stays clean from 204–215 µs of bit time, and the
slew skew correction turned out to be unnecessary altogether (it is measured and reported, but not
applied). If a future capture reports a bit time more than 5 % off 208.4 µs, the tool flags it and
`--bit-us` overrides.

`analog.csv` is 645 MB / 26.9M lines — stream it line by line, don't load it into memory, and
don't reach for numpy (not installed). Columns: `Time [s], RXD, TXD`.

## Wiring / capture setup

4-conductor thermostat-style link on `CN2`: `5V`, `0V`, `RXD`, `TXD`.

> **The physical terminal labels are the reverse of the line names used everywhere in this repo.**
> Established 2026-10-29 with the rev A board: wired label-to-label, the wall control's commands
> arrived on the board's TXD tap and its RXD tap saw nothing. The conductor on the terminal
> **labelled `TXD`** (at the ERV and at the wall control) carries the **commands** — CN2 sits on the
> wall control's PCB, so its labels are the panel's point of view. In this repo "RXD" always means
> the command line (wall control → ERV, 4.7 V) and "TXD" the state line (ERV → wall control), as
> in the 2025-12-14 capture, the decoder and the firmware.
>
> **So the rev A board's `RXD`/`TXD` screw terminals (J1 and J2) are wired crossed: the ERV's and
> the wall control's `TXD` terminal → board `RXD`, their `RXD` terminal → board `TXD`.** Do it on
> both J1 and J2, never one side only (the relay connects J1 straight to J2). Wired label-to-label,
> passthrough still works, but the 4.7 V commands land on the 1.16 V TXD tap, the 2.3 V state never
> reaches the 2.36 V RXD tap, and in intercept the command driver would fight the ERV's output.

Logic analyzer mapping used in the 2025-12-14 capture: `D2` = TXD (red), `D3` = RXD (orange),
ground to `0V`. In the CSV exports, column 1 = RXD, column 2 = TXD.

Capture timeline (t is capture-relative): ERV mains power-on ≈ 6.83 s, On/Off button press
≈ 14.24 s, capture ends 21.52 s.

## VERIFIED protocol

All of the following is confirmed against the analog capture with **zero** inconsistencies:
596/596 frames pass BCC, 7769/7769 bytes pass parity and stop-bit checks.

### Physical layer

| | RXD line | TXD line |
|---|---|---|
| Idle level | **0 V** | **0 V** |
| Active level | **4.7 V** | **2.3 V** |
| Bit time | 208.4 µs | 208.4 µs |

- **4800 baud, 8 data bits, even parity, 1 stop bit (8E1) — 11 bits/byte, INVERTED.**
- Inverted means idle = 0 V and the start bit is a **rising** edge. A normal (non-inverted)
  UART decode produces garbage — that is the single biggest error in the old notes.
- Bit time fit by requiring all pulse widths to be integer multiples of T: rms residual
  0.004 bits at 208.4 µs. Unambiguous.
- Edges are heavily RC-limited (~80 µs slew on a 208 µs bit). Threshold **at true mid-swing**
  (RXD ≈ 2.35 V, TXD ≈ 1.15 V) or you get a large duty-cycle skew and spurious framing errors.
- The 2.3 V swing on TXD is **below the ESP32 V_IH of ~2.48 V** — an ESP32 cannot reliably read
  this line directly. Needs a comparator or level shifter. (Open question: is 2.3 V the real
  driven level or an artifact of where the probe was placed?)

### Frame format

```
0x02 (STX) | A1 A0 | D7 D6 D5 D4 D3 D2 D1 D0 | 0x03 (ETX) | BCC
```

13 bytes total. `A1 A0` and `D7..D0` are **ASCII hex characters** ('0'–'9', 'A'–'F'), so the
payload is a 2-char address plus 4 data bytes.

**BCC = `~(XOR of every byte from A1 through ETX inclusive) & 0xFF`**

```python
def bcc(payload_bytes):          # payload_bytes = the 10 ASCII chars between STX and ETX
    x = 0
    for b in payload_bytes: x ^= b
    return (~(x ^ 0x03)) & 0xFF
```

Worked example: `02 '0' '0' 'F' 'F' 'F' 'F' 'F' 'F' 'F' 'F' 03 FC` →
`0x30^0x30 = 0`, eight `'F'` XOR to 0, `^0x03 = 0x03`, `~0x03 = 0xFC`. ✓

### Link timing

Both sides **free-run independently** at ~200 ms; there is no polling or arbitration. RXD period
200.1 ms, TXD period 200.2 ms, and they slowly drift against each other. Within a cycle, blocks
are spaced ~29.7 ms.

- **RXD sends 5 blocks/cycle**: addresses `00 10 20 30 F0`
- **TXD sends 4 blocks/cycle**: addresses `01 11 21 31`
- Once per second, TXD's slot carries `A1 B1 F1` (3 blocks) *instead of* `01 11 21 31`

Note the address low nibble encodes the direction: `0` on RXD, `1` on TXD.

### Direction (established, not assumed)

**RXD = wall control → unit (commands). TXD = unit → wall control (state).**

The labels are therefore from the **unit's** point of view, not the panel's.

Proof, from the On/Off press: RXD `00` changed to a new value and held it for exactly 3 cycles
(14.236, 14.436, 14.636 s), then **reverted** to its idle pattern. TXD `01` then latched that
same value **permanently**. A transient 3× burst that reverts is a command; the value that
latches is the resulting state.

### Observed register values

Command line (RXD), idle / "no request" pattern — note the FF staircase:

| addr | idle | during On/Off command (3 cycles) |
|---|---|---|
| `00` | `FFFFFF00` | `10303000` |
| `10` | `FFFF0000` | `20FF0000` |
| `20` | `FF000000` | `00000000` |
| `30` | `00000000` | `00000000` |
| `F0` | `00000000` | `00000000` |

At mains power-on (6.83 s) all five RXD registers send `FFFFFFFF` once, then the line goes quiet
for 2.1 s while the unit boots.

State line (TXD):

| addr | unit off | unit on (after press) |
|---|---|---|
| `01` | `00000000` | `10303000` |
| `11` | `00000000` | `20000000` |
| `21` | `00000000` | `00000000` |
| `31` | `00000000` | `00000000` |
| `A1` | `00000000` | `00000000` |
| `B1` | `41D13055` | `41D13055` |
| `F1` | `00003E00` | `00003E00` |

### Register map (decoded 2026-10-29)

**Data bytes are sent low nibble first.** The 8 ASCII chars are 4 bytes, each as two hex chars,
low nibble then high nibble: `"3103F0B4"` = `13 30 0F 4B`. Below, bytes are written as values.
Established from a display photo plus a timestamped button session through the rev A board:
`captures/2026-10-29/2026-10-29-notes.md` has every command and state change.

Each command register on RXD has a matching state register on TXD (`x0` → `x1`):

| Cmd → state | Byte 0 | Byte 1 | Byte 2 | Byte 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `00` → `01` | power (0 off, 1 on) | supply airflow code | exhaust airflow code | 0 |
| `10` → `11` | ASHRAE, tens of min/h | boost length, tens of min (state: non-zero only while boosting) | 0 | 0 |
| `20` → `21` | boost (1 on) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| — → `B1` | inside temp °C | inside %RH | outside temp °C | outside %RH |

- **Airflow code:** the wall control steps airflow in 20 CFM: 60, 80, … 180, 200. Those are codes
  1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 (CFM = 50 + 10 × code) and then **200 = code 16**, not 15. Seen on the bus:
  1, 3, 7, 9, 13, 16. Even codes and 14/15 are never sent by the panel; whether the ERV accepts
  them is untested.
- **ASHRAE:** set in 10 min/h steps; the byte is the value in tens (seen 1, 2, 3).
- **`B1`**: all four fields confirmed against the display. Outside: 2025-12-14 3 °C/85 %;
  2026-10-29 15 °C/75 %, 14 °C/76 %. Inside (In/Out button): 2026-10-29 66 °F/48 % = `13 30`
  (19 °C/48 %). Whole degrees Celsius; the panel converts to °F. Sub-zero encoding (assumed
  signed) unseen.
- **Commands:** `FF` = leave this byte alone. Each byte is held for 3 cycles (~0.6 s) on its own,
  so fields edited in quick succession overlap within one frame. The ERV applies a command within
  0.2–0.4 s of its first frame.
- **Off forgets, boost doesn't.** While off, the ERV zeroes `01` and `11`, and turning on makes the
  panel re-send a full snapshot (`00 = 10707000`, `10 = 30FF0000`, `20 = 00000000`). Boost is
  different: **the ERV times it itself and restores the pre-boost airflow on its own.** A 60 min
  boost started 22:36:22.48 ended at 23:36:24.59 (60 min 2 s) with `21`/`11` cleared and `01` back
  to 120/120, and nothing at all on RXD. The panel's countdown is display only (`11`/`21` stay
  constant while it runs). Cancelling early is the panel's job: it sends `20 = 00000000` plus an
  explicit airflow restore in `00`.
- **UI-only buttons:** Vent opens the airflow editor (each field sends its command when you move
  past it: supply, then exhaust, even if unchanged). A short Filter press sends nothing.

Still unknown: `30`/`F0` commands and `31`/`A1`/`F1` state never changed (`F1 = 00003E00`
throughout); the 3 s Filter reset, sub-zero outside temps
and the ASHRAE range.

## Superseded claims from the first attempt

README.md has been rewritten to match the verified decode, so these errors survive only in git
history (commit `6e51cac`) and in the stale `rxd.txt` / `txd.txt` / `digital.csv` exports. Listed
here so they aren't accidentally reintroduced from those files:

1. "RXD ~9600 baud" — no. **Both lines are 4800 baud.**
2. "non-inverted (idle high)" — no. **Both lines are inverted, idle low (0 V).**
3. "8N1" — no. **8E1** (even parity); 11 bits/byte, confirmed by the 2.29 ms byte pitch.
4. "signal swing ~1.2 V to ~3.8 V, treat as 3.3 V TTL" — no. RXD is **0–4.7 V (5 V logic)**;
   TXD is **0–2.3 V**. The 1.2–3.8 V figures were an artifact of bad thresholds on slow edges.
5. Decoded byte streams like `FC C7`, `3F AE E6 EE`, `2E F9` are **decode artifacts** and carry
   no meaning. Same for the "framing errors" attributed to oscillator tolerance — the real cause
   was wrong polarity and off-centre thresholds.
6. "Directionality to be determined" — resolved, see above.

The first attempt's hardware observations (connector pinout, PCB markings, button inventory) were
sound and are retained in README.md.

## Conventions for this repo

- Store new captures as `captures/YYYY-MM-DD/` with a `YYYY-MM-DD-notes.md` operator log.
- **Always log wall-clock or capture-relative timestamps for every button press.** The existing
  notes were timed by counting in the operator's head, which cost real effort to reconcile
  against the capture (the press is at 14.24 s, not the noted ~10 s).
- Export analog, not just digital. Digital exports bake in threshold choices that turned out to
  be wrong.
- When decoding, verify against BCC + parity + stop bits. All three at 100 % is the bar; anything
  less means the framing parameters are off, not that the bus is noisy.
